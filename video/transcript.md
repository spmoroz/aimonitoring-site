# Transcript: 4.5 years of AI in everyday radiology (3 min)

Narrated by Sergey Morozov. Explainer of Morozov et al., J Am Coll Radiol 2026, doi:10.1016/j.jacr.2026.09.026.

Hello, I am Sergey Morozov. For four and a half years, the 3R Swiss Imaging Network has used AI in everyday radiology. Here's what we learned. When a computer starts thinking slower than we do, we replace it. So what about an AI whose answer arrives after the report is signed? At the 3R Swiss Imaging Network, we measured exactly that in routine work. Four and a half years, 20 centers, 10 AI tools from seven vendors, about 389,000 AI-assisted exams. Everyone asks if AI is accurate. Few ask if it arrives in time. And actually used by radiologists. The design: three data sources, triangulated. The AI platform dashboard, 96,874 exams. The radiology information system, 20,909 reports with and without AI used. And 58 radiologists surveyed twice. Three clocks. Clock one, AI latency. 2.06 minutes, median. The algorithm needs about 0. 2. 72% is data routing. A sprinter stuck in traffic. Clock two: Does the result arrive in time? 80.6% arrive before the report is started. 7.2% arrive after it is signed. 1,584 exams, a first of its kind to our knowledge. Clock three: The radiologist report time. When the result is there in time, it pays. Reports 26% faster in trauma radiography, 18 in knee MRI, adjusted for the radiologist. Trauma radiography alone, an estimated 0.69 of a radiologist a year. That is roughly a three to one return on investment for the platform. Two to one after adjustment. On top of the clocks, we asked the radiologists. 91% use AI and 66% use AI regularly. They rate their AI tools 7.28 out of 10. Yet perceived productivity stays at 2.94 out of 5. Economists call it the Jevons paradox. Saved time may turn into more cases. From this triangulation come the monitoring metrics. Three clocks per tool, every quarter. We also propose a standardized radiologist questionnaire. Push studies to AI on completion, put time to display targets in contracts with AI vendors and distributors. And then the paradox. Today, AI can't keep up with the radiologist. Fix that, and with rising volumes, the radiologist may not keep up with AI. Will we still be able to monitor what it does? So we must monitor it and prepare radiologists to work with it. AI should run like a Swiss train, on time, comfortable, and taking us exactly where we need to go. Right result, right moment.
